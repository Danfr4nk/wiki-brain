# Libby Titus

Libby Titus — **Libby Fagen** in the corpus, born Elizabeth Jurist, the
songwriter who wrote *"Love Has No Pride"* with Eric Kaz and who was married to
Donald Fagen of Steely Dan for thirty-one years — employed
[[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]] as a paid personal assistant in Manhattan
through the first half of **2024**. She was terminally ill for most of it. She
died on **13 October 2024, aged 77**, with an unpaid-wages dispute between them
still open.

Dan never met her. She exists in this corpus entirely through Annie's running
commentary — 116 messages — and she is the best-documented thing about Annie's
working life anywhere in the archive.

She matters here for four reasons, none of them about her. She is the **largest
documented income source on Annie's side** of the relationship; she **falsifies a
standing claim** this wiki carried about Annie's employment; the dispute that
ended the arrangement is the corpus's clearest instance of
[[wiki/mind/concepts/forensic-method|Dan's forensic register]] pointed outward on
somebody else's behalf; and her death removes that income five months before
Annie's documented collapse begins.

> **Sensitivity note.** This page concerns a named, identifiable person's final
> illness, her household finances and a wage dispute with her estate's
> predecessor, recorded from one side of a private conversation she was not party
> to and cannot correct. She has surviving family. It is kept because it is
> load-bearing for the biography this wiki is about, and written to that standard
> and no further. **It remains a candidate for the portal seal**
> (`wiki.locks.json`, portal repo); the operator's decision, still outstanding.

## The arc, February to October 2024

The work is bounded much more tightly than the mention count suggests: **112 of
the 116 messages fall between February and mid-August 2024.** Everything after is
aftermath.

**February — the trial.** It opens on **2024-02-08** with *"Libby asked me to come
again tomorrow lol"*, implying a prior visit the corpus does not hold. The
register is set immediately and never changes: the work and the money arrive in
the same sentence. *"I am taking Libby food from Antonuccis. So hopefully she will
give me something for bringing her food"* (02-11). *"I gotta gotta gotta get up
early tomorrow and help Libby. I need money."* (02-16).

**March — the illness, and the job turning medical.** On **2024-03-05** Annie
reports that Libby has told her she has brain cancer, and *"Libby just said 'I
think I'm dying..'"*; the next day, that it is also back in her right lung. From
there Annie is driving her to appointments, sitting through them (*"we are waiting
to see doctor now"*, 03-11), taking her for blood tests before a scheduled
procedure (03-17), and spending half-hours on the phone with doctors between
shifts (03-19). On **03-20** Libby emails her **cemetery plots**.

**May to July — the peak, and the second job.** The volume is highest in May
(25 mentions) and July (29). Annie is working both jobs — *"Now I'll be here 6
days a week and then Libby in mornings…: I don't want to get worked again and pass
out like last time"* (05-08) — and the Libby money is earmarked in the messages
for specific purchases, most often phones and phone service.

**August — the break.** On **2024-08-06**: *"after I had gotten screamed at by
Libby you get mad over Amber taking me for ICECREAM."* Two days later the
household is a location rather than a person: *"Aka where Libby and Donald live
lol."* The work stops.

## The unpaid wages, and the letters Dan wrote

This is the part the first version of the page missed entirely, and it is the
most consequential thing on it.

Through August 2024 Annie was owed money, was **asked to sign an NDA**, and heard
nothing. **Dan drafted three escalating demand letters for her**, all of them
sent from his handle into the thread for her to use:

> **2024-08-01** — *"Dear x I'm reaching out to inform you of the unpaid wages
> which I have been unable to receive and request a date that I can be
> compensated. The total amount is x (y hours at **$75/hour**)…"*
>
> **2024-08-13, 00:18** — *"…I wanted to follow up with you about the unpaid wages
> that I had previously reached out to you about on (date of last email) as I had
> not received any response."*
>
> **2024-08-13, 01:00** — ***"Final Request for Payment of Unpaid Wages"***, with
> *"I would like to resolve this issue without any additional complications and am
> not interested in seeking further remedy from **Mr or Mrs Fagen**. Subsequently
> I have even agreed to sign…"*

Annie's own position on the NDA is stated once and it is a trade: *"As you know I
was asked to sign an NDA. which I have not heard anything about. And also put at
the end in exchange for signing an NDA I would like to receive the unpaid
wages"* (2024-08-01). She forwards the correspondence and the reply to Libby's
own address on 08-13, and on 08-14 adds the detail that fixes the rate: *"the
hourly rate was set by Libby Fagen."*

**The money was not abstract.** *"I may try to see if John will hold this weeks
rent check and explain to him the situation that I am waiting for a check from
Libby"* (2024-08-01) — [[wiki/people/john-paci|John]] being the landlord at
[[wiki/places/307-e-76th-st]].

**Nothing in the corpus records a resolution.** The last letter is 13 August. She
died on 13 October. On the evidence available the dispute was open when she died,
and this page will not assume otherwise in either direction.

> **This is the corpus's clearest instance of the forensic register turned
> outward on somebody else's behalf.** Dated correspondence, an itemised
> hours-times-rate total, an explicit statement of what remedy is *not* being
> sought, a final-notice escalation on a fixed interval — it is the same
> instrument [[wiki/mind/concepts/forensic-method]] describes him turning on his
> own relationships, and one of very few times in eleven years it is deployed for
> a third party's material benefit rather than to adjudicate a private grievance.
> Whether it worked is unknown. That he wrote it is not.

> **CONTRADICTION: the rate does not reconcile.** The demand letters state
> **$75/hour**, set by Libby. But on 2024-05-17 Annie writes *"I was literally
> with Libby for just over 3 hours today.. she paid me 500"* — roughly $167/hour —
> and a week later invites Dan to guess the figure for four hours. Both are
> first-hand and neither is retracted. Possible readings: the rate was raised or
> lowered between May and August; the $500 included back pay or a gift; or the
> $75 figure is the one Annie could evidence in writing and the cash payments were
> informal. **Unresolved, and recorded rather than smoothed.**

## What it corrects about Annie

**The standing account of Annie's employment is wrong for 2024.**
`raw/self/dox-md/Honest assessment and value judgment analysis.md` states she
*"was fired in 2023 and spent a full year unemployed,"* and the framing
propagated. She was working **two jobs** through the first half of 2024, at a
documented rate above anything on record for Dan in the same period, and when the
money stopped she pursued it formally. Corrected on [[wiki/people/annie-ulmer]].

The **$119K–$123K net outflow** at [[wiki/mind/synthesis/estate-money-spine]] is
unaffected — it is payment-app derived, not employment-derived — but the
single-earner reading of 2024 is not what the record supports, and the inbound
side of those exports has still never been swept.

## The texture, which is still worth keeping

The soured ending does not cancel what came before it, and the record of the
middle months is unusual for this archive: Annie is reliable, turns up, and is
trusted with money, medical appointments and correspondence by someone who
plainly liked her. It is also funny. Libby shows her a brooch and remarks *"I did
cocaine once, but I didn't like it"* (05-22). At an appointment she asks the woman
at the front desk where she can get her hat — it is a hijab — and on being told,
replies *"oh with my past I probably shouldn't"* (03-22). She fires her other
helper (05-02), takes against a replacement named Janine (05-10), gives Annie a
pair of loafers (05-09), and cc's her on so much correspondence that Annie ends up
looking at a spreadsheet of the household bank accounts and says she felt wrong
doing it (05-30).

Both things are true and the page holds them together: a woman dying of brain
cancer who liked the person helping her, and an employment relationship that
ended with a screaming match, an NDA, and three unanswered letters.
