# David J. Frank

David J. Frank is Dan's paternal great-grandfather and the person the whole
paternal line's geography starts from. He was born in Russia on 12 August 1892,
was living in Manhattan by 1900 as a child of the Eastern European Jewish
immigration, and stayed in New York City for at least fifteen years — Manhattan
in 1900, 1905 and 1910, the Bronx on 1 June 1915 — before appearing in
Brownsville, Fayette County, Pennsylvania in the 1920 census. He remained in the
county for the last forty years of his life, died in Brownsville on 6 April 1960,
and was buried in Hopwood. The New York chapter is the part of his record the
wiki previously did not carry, and it is the part that matters most: the founder
of the line did not arrive in Fayette County, he **relocated** to it, out of New
York City, which is exactly the move his great-grandson would later make twice.

His record also carries a residence entry for Uniontown dated **1993–1997** —
thirty-three years after his death. That is a mis-attached city-directory
listing, almost certainly a namesake or a surviving-household entry, and it is
noted here so that a later reader does not treat it as evidence of anything. The
515-individual tree contains enough automated source-matching that entries of
this kind should be expected rather than trusted.

## What the New York years do to the Fayette rule

[[wiki/mind/synthesis/fayette-return]] originally scored G1 as "the anchor
rather than an instance," on the reasoning that an immigrant arriving is not a
person returning. That reasoning was correct as far as it went and it was built
on an incomplete record: it did not know that the arrival had a fifteen-year New
York City stop in the middle of it.

The correction does not make David an instance of leaving-and-returning — he had
nowhere in Fayette to return *to*. What it does is establish that the county was
**chosen over New York City** at the very first opportunity the family had to
choose anything, and that the specific migration Dan performs in
[[wiki/timeline/periods/feb-apr-2025-return-and-rupture]] — leaving New York for
Fayette County — is the same vector the line was founded on. Dan's returns are
usually read as retreat. The founder's identical move is not available to be read
that way, because he had never lived in Fayette County before making it.

His burial place carries the other half of the finding. David died in
Brownsville and was buried in Hopwood; [[wiki/people/sadie-harris]] was buried in
Hopwood in 1997; [[wiki/people/morley-frank]] died and was buried in Hopwood in
1998. The rule's terminus resolves to one small town's cemetery across three
generations, only one of whom died there.

## Gaps

- **The immigration itself is undocumented on disk.** No passenger manifest,
  naturalization record or arrival date for David appears in the export as
  extracted; "born Russia 1892, in Manhattan by 1900" is the whole of it.
- **The reason for the move is unknown.** Nothing explains why a New York family
  went to a Fayette County coal town between 1915 and 1920 — work, kin, or a
  business opportunity are all plausible and none is attested.
- **Occupation unrecorded.** The extraction pulled no trade or employer for him,
  which leaves the family's economic footing in its first Fayette decades blank.
