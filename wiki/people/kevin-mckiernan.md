# Kevin McKiernan

Kevin McKiernan was Dan's employer — company president at
[[wiki/work/creative-license|Creative License]], a celebrity-talent and
licensed-music brokering company at 71 8th Ave in the West Village — from
April 2011 to February 2012. He is the best-documented antagonist anywhere
in the corpus below the 2026 BFS Foods dispute. Dan cited a travel-expense
billing irregularity as his stated reason for leaving, but the
better-documented conduct is broader: using an employee's own account to
pressure former staff, altering departing interns' contracts to silence
them, and backing a false, notarized statement to try to erase a
whistleblowing former employee's professional identity.

## What Dan says he cited when he quit

Per Dan's own later recollection (in a 2026 AI chat session, quoted at
[[wiki/work/creative-license]]), what he told McKiernan at the time was a
"small fraud with airplane tickets" — some irregularity in McKiernan's
travel-expense billing that Dan, whose job included "oversight of office
and employee expenses," was positioned to notice. Dan's own framing is
self-doubting rather than triumphant: he used it as concrete "legal
ground" because a personality complaint would have been dismissed, and he
explicitly wonders, years later, whether he "blew it up into a much much
bigger thing." The specific mechanism of the alleged fraud is not
independently verified anywhere in the corpus; only the fact that Dan
cited it is.

## The payroll dispute (Feb–Apr 2012)

McKiernan is only lightly present in the six-week final-paycheck fight
that opens the record of the relationship's end — most of the back-and-forth
runs through [[wiki/people/marty-jackson|Marty Jackson]] in accounting —
but he is copied throughout, and his one direct instruction on the
thread ("Send it back," re: the unsigned, wrong-amount final check) is
terse in a way that reads as delegated indifference rather than
engagement. Dan filed IRS and NYS Department of Labor complaints over a
disputed $104 MetroCard charge before the matter closed; McKiernan never
personally responds to any of the substantive back-and-forth in the
recovered thread.

## The whistleblower disclosure (May 2012)

The relationship's real substance is in what Dan told a former coworker,
[[wiki/people/renae-holland|Renae Holland]], three months after leaving:
that in December 2011, while Dan was still employed, McKiernan had used
Dan's own email and LinkedIn account to send recommendation-removal
requests to former employees — Renae among them — without Dan writing the
emails himself and over Dan's own objection. Dan's account, verbatim: "I
(my account, rather) was acting as a channel for Kevin to act out his most
immature fantasies." The more serious claim in the same email: McKiernan
had Dan send **altered employment contracts to two former interns**, with
a clause quietly added "that prohibited recommendations without CEO
approval" — a mechanism specifically designed to prevent departing junior
staff from speaking honestly about the company afterward. Renae's reply
corroborates independently, from her own time at the company: "in my time
there I was Kevin falsify more documents that I can count — I know what he
is capable of."

## Retaliation against the whistleblower (June 2012)

A month after Dan's disclosure to Renae, McKiernan filed a formal
intellectual-property complaint against Dan's own LinkedIn profile,
asserting Dan had never been a Creative License employee and had
fabricated his job title — backed by two documents notarized by
[[wiki/people/katherine-palakovich|Katherine Palakovich]], a CL employee,
using her own notary commission on McKiernan's behalf against a former
coworker whose employment McKiernan's own accounting department had spent
the previous two months corresponding with in writing. Dan's counter-notice
named the motive directly: "Mr. McKiernan has and continues to attempt to
smear my name because of my actions as a whistleblower while under his
employ." Whether LinkedIn ultimately sided with Dan is not confirmed by
any resolution email in the recovered record, though his account appears
to have survived.

## What doesn't fit the pattern

Despite the above, Dan kept McKiernan's contact information for close to a
decade (Facebook address-book snapshots 2016–2021 still carry
`kevin@creativelicense.com`), and the company's social circle — including
Katherine Palakovich — still had Dan on its "ex-CL employees" list as late
as December 2014. This page states the documented misconduct plainly and
does not let the contact-list detail soften it, but it is worth recording
rather than smoothing over: the professional relationship apparently never
formally severed on Dan's end, even after a conflict serious enough to
involve two government agencies and a false notarized statement.

## Gaps

Nothing is documented about McKiernan's life beyond this one-year
employment window and its aftermath — no age, no other ventures, no
account of the company's ultimate fate (it was still operating as of
Katherine's December 2014 email). What specifically motivated the
December 2011 use of Dan's account, or what "triggered" his attack on
Renae per her own phrasing, is unrecovered. The exact notarized statements
he submitted to LinkedIn (`Creative License 2.pdf`) were not extracted in
