# Morley Jay Frank

Morley Jay Frank is Dan's paternal grandfather and the single generation that
turns a personal habit into a family rule. Born in Brownsville, Fayette County
on 20 August 1927 to the immigrant couple [[wiki/people/david-j-frank]] and
[[wiki/people/sadie-harris]], he is the first Frank with a Fayette County to
come *back* to rather than move to — and he is the only member of the line
before Dan who is documented leaving it and returning. He appears in Seattle,
Washington in 1957 and in Uniontown by 1959, then spends the remaining forty
years in the Hopwood/Uniontown/Champion cluster, dying in Hopwood on 13 December
1998 and being buried there. His public-record footprint is unusually heavy for
the era — censuses, Brownsville High yearbooks for 1943, 1944 and 1945,
Selective Service and military records, Social Security, hospital admissions,
Find A Grave and newspaper items.

## The Seattle window, now bracketed

[[wiki/mind/synthesis/fayette-return]] named Morley's Seattle duration as one of
its three open gaps, on the grounds that a single 1957 datum was all that
attested it and that a brief posting rather than a relocation would have removed
the only pre-Dan instance of the rule. The residence table above does not close
that gap but it narrows it from one side and hardens the more important half:
**the return is dated.** The last Brownsville record is 1945, Seattle is 1957,
Uniontown is 1959. Whatever the stay's length, the turnaround from the Seattle
datum to a Fayette County address is at most two years — the tightest in the
line's record, against Dan's own returns of roughly two years from Florida and
seven from New York.

What remains open is the front end. The years 1946–1956 carry no residence entry
at all, so Seattle could have run anywhere from months to a decade, and the
military records of 1945 raise the ordinary possibility that the westward move
was service-related rather than elective. That distinction is the live falsifier
and it is not settled by anything on disk.

## Champion, and a coincidence worth recording

From 1996 through 1999 the directories list Morley at **Champion,
Pennsylvania**, concurrently with — not instead of — a Hopwood address. Read as
a second address rather than a relocation, it is the first documented residence
outside Fayette County anywhere in the line since his father's New York years,
and it is the closest the record comes to a falsifier for the Fayette rule
without actually being one: he dies and is buried in Hopwood regardless.

Champion is the resort town at Seven Springs. `CONTEXT_CORE_EXPANDED.md` records
Dan's origin period (1988–2008) as carrying a **ski identity — "Seven Springs,
every weekend"** — and the Champion listings fall inside it, when Dan was eight
to eleven years old. A family address in the resort town during exactly the
childhood years the ski habit is recorded is a documented co-occurrence and
nothing stronger; no source connects the grandfather's second address to the
grandson's weekends, and the inference should not be made silently. It is
recorded here because it is checkable and nobody has checked it.

## The one personal record

Dan's cousin [[wiki/people/alex-frank]] wrote an essay recounting Morley's
funeral that includes the detail of Eat'n Park delivering "Frownie" cookies —
the frowning-face counterpart to the chain's famous Smiley Cookie — to the
gathering. It is dark, specific, small-town Western Pennsylvania texture, and
Dan still references it nearly three decades later ("i swear to god lmao,"
quoting the essay in a 2025 message). It is also the only surviving personal
account of Morley anywhere in the corpus; everything else on this page is a
coordinate.

## Gaps

- **1946–1956 is blank.** No residence, employment or marriage record covers the
  decade between the Brownsville yearbooks and Seattle, which is precisely the
  window that would settle how long the departure lasted.
- **The Seattle move's cause is unknown** — service posting, work, or elective
  relocation are all consistent with what is on disk.
- **Champion's county is unverified here.** The directory gives the town only.
  Confirming it lies outside Fayette County would make it the line's only
  attested out-of-county residence in four generations, which is worth the check.
