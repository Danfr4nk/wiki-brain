Dan's mother, and the second-largest presence in the entire corpus: **33,698
messages across ten years and nine months**, behind only
[[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]] (119,405 across four handles) and ahead of
every other person in it. She is a licensed realtor in Fayette County, the owner of the house Dan
grew up in and lived in again from 2025, and — on her own accounting in June
2020 — a person who earned **$11,000 to $14,000 a year for a decade against
$10,000 a year in property taxes.** The relationship's governing feature is
not affection or conflict, both of which are abundant, but **an account
between them that has never closed.** Each keeps a ledger of what the other
owes; each has produced itemised statements of it in writing; neither has ever
been settled by anything except the next emergency. The corpus's most recent
message from her, timestamped 11 August 2026, is on that subject.

## The ledger that never closes

The single most reproducible fact about this relationship is that both people
keep books on each other, and the books disagree.

The clearest artifact is a message Suz sent at 6:34 on the morning of 3
October 2018. It is not prose; it is an account statement.

> Dan to Suzanne
> $1000
> $14,000+
> Sum of transactions on app
>
> Suzanne to Dan (past week)
> $450
> If you can give me $1450, that erases the above and adds $1000 to what I
> borrowed from you. That way I can pay the other bills I owe and have a
> hundred or three to live on.

The $14,000 is the load-bearing number in the relationship, and it moved
**from Dan to Suz**, which is the opposite direction from the one this page
previously implied. In August 2018 — four months after
[[wiki/people/fran-coldren|Fran]] died and two years before the estate
actually distributed — Dan told her he was going to "take out $20k to float me
until the estate is wrapped up (fingers crossed) and I'm going to add your
number on top of that." He then set about reducing the frequency of the
transfers rather than the amount, for a reason he states plainly: *"I think
the optics would be bad if I'm calling once a month for money. Also that was
harmful to my grasp of the last money, once you start making a bunch of
smaller transfers it's easier to justify doing on a day to day basis."* Her
reply is the most practical financial advice anywhere in the thread, and it is
addressed to him:

> You probably should WRITE DOWN on paper what you absolutely need each month
> and know that number. Then maybe set up auto bill pay through their
> accounts… I don't know if you can handle an allowance. You will forget when
> bills are due and spend the money.

By June 2019 the $14,000 had become the thing they fought about. Dan: *"I LENT
YOU 14,000 … which i'm supposed to be paid back when the inheritance comes
right"* (20 June). By 3 July it had become a legal threat in both directions.
That day is one of the eight highest-volume days in the entire thread (105
messages) and it contains the whole mechanism in compressed form. Dan's
position: *"i want the money you were supposed to pay me when the estate goes
through."* Hers:

> I have the PayPal receipts from the $14,000 you stole from me. I have the
> counterfeit money you produced, I have all the bills I paid for in Florida,
> NY and at Va. you want to go to court over this. That's fine.

And, three hours later, the framing she returns to for the rest of the decade:

> $14,000 is a drop in the bucket compared to what you have cost me since
> 2004. You cannot disprove any of that and it's absolutely incredible how you
> ignore your own responsibility in this and want to turn around and blame ME.

Neither claim was ever adjudicated and neither was withdrawn. Dan says he
recovered $4,000 of it — *"thank goodness i got 4,000 of it back before you
decided you weren't paying me back"* — and no source records any further
repayment. The fight ends the way most of them end: two hours after *"Bye
Felicia!"* and *"NO FUCKING FORWARDING ADDRESS,"* she writes *"Just for a
minute, try to put yourself in my position if you can,"* and then, at 19:54,
*"Was that an accident or do you need to talk?"*

## The bankruptcy, and why 337 Saratoga was sold

This is the correction that reorganises the last two years of the page. The
sale of Dan's childhood home was not a decision his mother made. It was the
remedy in her own bankruptcy.

On 30 March 2026 Dan pasted a case audit into the thread. The document is
model-generated — it carries the glyph formatting of his AI sessions and must
be read as **AI-secondary**, with the caveat that its inputs were evidently
things she had told him — but it is specific in a way that is checkable from
outside the corpus, and no other source in `raw/` contradicts it:

- **Case #24-22285-GLT**, Chapter 13 voluntary petition, **filed October 2024**
- Scheduled liabilities **~$157,000**
- Primary creditors: **IRS priority claims for tax years 2018, 2019, 2020 and
  2021**, plus PA Department of Revenue arrears
- Plan payments of **$300/month**, with the plan depending on liquidating 337
- 341 meeting of creditors, November 2024
- 337 Saratoga **owned free and clear, no mortgage** — the sole mechanism for
  resolving the debt
- Asking price **$615,000 (May 2025)**, adjusted to $500–550k; a June 2025
  contract terminated over inspection and pest reports
- A court **"drop dead" provision in August 2025**: without a binding sales
  agreement, automatic conversion to Chapter 7 or dismissal

Her own messages bracket it on both sides. On 4 September 2024, before filing:
*"I don't even think bankruptcy is going to work for me."* On 8 November 2024,
after: *"I need insurance that doesn't bankrupt me. Oh! Wait! I already AM
bankrupt!🤭."* On 22 June 2026, a week before the closing: *"I have to have a
hearing."* The 2018 precedent is on the record too — *"until that sells, I'm
in danger of having to file bankruptcy. That would mean I'll be moving in with
you, whoever you go"* (24 May 2018) — which makes the 2024 filing the end of a
six-year approach rather than a shock.

The consequence for reading the rest of the corpus is direct. The buyers
(Jennifer J. and Scott P. Delverm, $465k, closing 23 June 2026, seven-day
vacate) were not chosen for price; a bankruptcy sale optimises for certainty
of closing, because the alternative is a trustee who takes fees out of the
equity first. The "bankruptcy approval" contingency the
[[wiki/places/337-saratoga-drive|337 page]] records is a Motion to Sell with a
21-day creditor-objection window. And the reason the asking price fell from
$615k to $465k over thirteen months is that the house needed work she had no
money to do while the IRS claims accrued interest daily.

### The colonization reading

The terminal-phase dossiers name Suz as the target of a deliberate
image-management effort: Annie maintained a warm, attentive persona with her —

The wiki presents that as the dossiers' reading rather than a settled finding,

warmth between Suz and Annie is documented from December 2015, ten years before

Whatever the terminal-phase persona was, it was built on a decade of real
relationship, which is what made it usable.

## What would change this reading

The page argues that the relationship is best understood as an unsettled
mutual account rather than a one-directional dependency, and that her financial
capacity has been systematically overstated in the wiki. Two things would
falsify that:

- **A documented net flow from her to him that exceeds the $14,000.** The
  standing claim would be that she gave more than she received; the record so
  far shows the single largest transfer running the other way and the
  aggregate uncertain. Bank or PayPal records, or the "PayPal receipts" she
  says she holds, would settle it.
- **Income or asset records inconsistent with $11–14k/year.** Her own statement
  is testimony, made in an argument with an HOA, and could be low. The Chapter
  13 schedules — public, and filed under a case number this page now names —
  contain the real figure.

The page also predicts that the housing question resolves against Dan rather
than jointly: the 11 August 2026 message is the second time in eighteen months
his residence has depended on a decision she was not free to make on his behalf.
