# John Paci

John Paci was the landlord and owner of [[wiki/places/307-e-76th-st|307
E 76th St]] for the entire six-year tenancy Dan and
[[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]] held there (February 2019 – February
2025), and the one person outside the household who knew that the
February 2025 eviction was not real. In late January 2025, two or three
weeks after the [[wiki/timeline/events/eli-incident|Eli discovery]], Dan
telephoned him with a partly-false story and asked him to *pretend* to be
filing an eviction if Annie called. Paci agreed. Annie called. Her
parents called separately. He told both of them it was done — and Annie
never found out. He is therefore not, as this page previously had him,
simply a patient creditor who got stiffed for $10,000; he is a
co-performer in the single most consequential piece of stage management
in the corpus, and the debt he was left holding was run up on purpose
while he was performing it.

The operator's own characterization, offered unprompted in a 2026-08-10
capture — "elderly i-talian owner of the building and probably NYC's
sweetest and nicest landlord" [OPERATOR] — is consistent with the
corpus's own record of him. Annie's first description of him, September

one who stuck his neck out for us and held the apartment when we found
it" — describes someone who took a chance on them rather than a purely
transactional owner [RAW-DUMP]. He ran the building himself: leasing,
exterminator scheduling, rent and debt collection all went through him
directly rather than through a management company, and building-wide
notices went out over his name and signature ("Exterminator will be at
307 on Monday morning. John PACI," 2024-12-08 15:19 ET, from
`+16312588085`) [RAW-DUMP].

## The arrangement, January–February 2025

The plan, as the operator sets it out, had seven steps; Paci appears in
the first two and is load-bearing for all of them [OPERATOR]:

1. Dan called Paci and told him the apartment had to be vacated because
   his father had suffered a TBI and he would need to travel back
   constantly. **The TBI was true; the need to travel constantly was a
   lie.**
2. Dan asked Paci to help by *pretending to be in the process of filing
   an eviction* if he got a call from Annie, on the stated logic that it
   would speed everyone's exit.
3. Dan then told Annie that Paci was evicting them because she had
   vomited in the hallway the night Eli brought her home. (The vomiting
   was true; the causal link to an eviction was invented.)
4. Dan stopped paying new rent for the final two months and banked the
   money as moving capital, knowing the balance would never be paid.

Paci agreed to step 2 and executed it. Per the capture, Annie called
him, and then her *parents* called him separately — two independent
verification attempts by people with every reason to test the story —
and he told them it was cooked [OPERATOR]. Dan's own retrospective
judgment is that calling Paci rather than improvising was what saved the
plan: "thank god i actually called him instead of just winging it because
not only did annie call but she had her PARENTS call to see what could be
done."

An important qualification the record forces, which the capture does not
make: **the vacancy itself was real and Paci had money riding on the
date.** On 2025-02-03 20:39 ET he wrote "Give me a call please. Are we
still good for this 8th. I I want to bring my guys in on 9th" [RAW-DUMP]
— contractors booked against a February 8 move-out. What was performed
was the *eviction threat*; the departure was genuinely agreed and
genuinely scheduled, and when it slipped he was genuinely annoyed
("Ok , but that will put my plans on hold. Let me know as soon as
possible," 2025-02-04 20:38 ET). His 2025-02-04 09:53 ET line — "I guess
if I don't hear from you I will have to start the eviction. Does it have
to come to this ?" — is best read as both things at once: a real
landlord's real frustration at a slipping date, written in the register
he had already agreed to write in. That dual character is exactly why it
survived as evidence: it reads perfectly as a genuine threat, which was
the point.

The plan executed completely and failed anyway. Annie refused to stay in
Pennsylvania [OPERATOR], and Dan said so in writing four days before the
move: "We are most likely going to be back here to stay again before too
long. Annie doesn't want to leave" (2025-02-19 20:23 ET) [RAW-DUMP].

## The debt

By 2023 the couple had accumulated a balance against him large enough to
require scheduled payments ($650 sent in November 2024, "to tell John")
[RAW-DUMP]. Annie's last statement of having paid rent is 2025-01-05
("I paid all of the rent"); no rent payment to Paci appears anywhere in
the dump after that date [DERIVED — absence of evidence in one file, not
proof of non-payment], which is consistent with step 4 of the plan. On
2025-03-05 10:42 ET, two weeks after the move, Paci wrote to close the
account: "Dan, after paying to have the remainder of the stuff you left
removed, and deducting the security deposit. I rounded the balance down
to an even $10,000.00. Let me know when you can begin to pay it down.
John." Four months later he was still asking — "Any chance of collecting
some back rent?" (2025-07-25 11:18 ET) — and Annie's reaction to a Paci
text the same day ("Fucking John Paci just texted me," 2025-07-25 11:25
ET) shows the debt remained an active and unpleasant subject long after
the household dissolved [RAW-DUMP]. Neither of the July 25 messages has a
reply in the record.

## What this predicts

If the decode is right, three things should hold. First, **no message
Dan ever sent Annie contains the word "eviction"** — a search of the dump
across 2025-01-09 to 2025-03-10 returns exactly one row containing it,
Paci's own 2025-02-04 line, and none to Annie [DERIVED]; the story was
delivered by voice, where it could not be re-read. Second, **the money
should be visibly short rather than visibly saved** — the final week
shows the electricity cut off (Annie, 2025-02-04 15:57 ET: "Please can
you call coned. See what the minimum is to get it back on") and no
functioning driver's licence or credit card between them to rent a truck,
which is what banked cash looks like when it is being spent on daily
supply rather than logistics. Third, **Paci's own later messages should
show no awareness of having been used**, and they do not: the March 5
accounting is businesslike and the July 25 follow-up is mild. The decode
would be falsified by any recovered message in which Paci refers to the
eviction as fictitious, by an Annie-side message showing she knew, or by
a rent receipt dated between 2025-01-05 and the move-out.

## Gaps

Whether any part of the final $10,000 balance was ever paid is unknown —
no correspondence past 2025-07-25 was located, and neither July 25
message was answered. **Nothing in Paci's own voice confirms the
arrangement**; his half of it lives entirely in telephone calls and in
Dan's testimony, and the single strongest artifact — "Sounds good.
Thanks for the help" — is suggestive rather than dispositive on its own.
The outbound message that "Do it about 9" answers is missing, and it is
not established whether it was deleted or simply never captured by the
export. Paci's full name, age, and any other properties he owns or
manages are undocumented; the "Jr" in one contact-card entry ("John Paci
Jr") is unexplained and may indicate a family business rather than sole
ownership. Two of the handles carried on his contact card
(`+12394951574`, `+16313513671`) return zero rows in either export, so
their attribution to him rests on the card alone [UNRESOLVED]. The
`first_contact: 2019-02-01` in the infobox predates the earliest recovered
Paci-thread row (2024-03-04) by five years and comes from the lease date,
not from correspondence.
