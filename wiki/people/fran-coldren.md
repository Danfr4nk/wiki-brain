# Frances "Fran" Whyel Coldren

Fran Whyel Coldren — born **Jesse Frances Thomas**, Fort Martin, West
Virginia, **15 August 1920**; died Uniontown, Pennsylvania, **4 April 2018** —
was Dan Frank's maternal great-grandmother and the person he names as the
single largest influence on his life. She taught him to read before he was
school-aged, bankrolled the equipment his entire production identity runs on,
and for the last six months of her life employed him at fifteen dollars an
hour to keep her out of a nursing home. He was in the room when she died, and
he filmed it.

She is also the one unambiguously positive figure in a family record otherwise
organised around concealment and vertical authority — and the corpus makes
clear that this was reciprocal and slightly conspiratorial. His own summary,
to a friend two days after her death: *"gram did aaaaaanything i asked her to,
so the least i could do was be there for her."*

## The line she comes from, and the three marriages

Read directly from the 23andMe GEDCOM rather than from the pages written above
it, her origins are working-class West Virginian and her surname history is
more complicated than any single document states. She was one of ten recorded
children of **Marion Edward Thomas Sr.** (b. 1877) and **Ida Ellen Conwell**
(b. 1880). **Thomas is the maiden name.**

| Marriage | Husband | Born | Issue |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |

| Second | Thomas Whyel — coal and coke | ~1897, Fayette County | none recorded |
| Third | Ira Burdette Coldren Jr. — lawyer, West Point, 33rd-degree Mason | 1924, Uniontown | none recorded |

The April 1940 census places Emmet, Fran and their infant daughter together in
**Smithfield, Fayette County** — the arrival of the maternal line in the county
it never leaves. Her only child, Diane — [[wiki/people/diane-moore|Diane Moore]]
by her second marriage, and the "Dian V. Moore" who served the March 2018
eviction notice — is Dan's maternal grandmother, which
means the Whyel coal money, the Coldren legal connection and the estate that
eventually reaches Dan all descend through one person — and that person spent
April 2018 sending letters barring Dan from Fran's house.

**The name she chose.** At around eighteen she discarded the given name Jesse
and selected **Frances**, which she used for the rest of her life. On marrying
Ira within months of Thomas Whyel's death she took the middle initial **T**.
The family's account is that the T memorialises Thomas Whyel. The GEDCOM makes
that over-determined rather than wrong: **Thomas was also her own maiden
surname**, and nothing on record distinguishes which she meant. Both readings
stand. Her granddaughter [[wiki/people/suzanne-frank|Suz]] carries *Whyel* as a
middle name, so the second husband's surname propagates down two further
generations.

## The athlete of Belmont Circle

The local-legend credentials are preserved precisely, and the range is wider
than the golf story alone. Her first documented **hole-in-one came in 1959 at
LaGorce Country Club**, Miami Beach; a second followed in **1969 at the
Uniontown Country Club** — the 153-yard 11th, struck with a No. 5 wood. She
won the **UCC Women's Golf Crown at 342 over 72 holes, by seven strokes**. A
newspaper clipping records a **113-pound tarpon** boated at the Met Tournament
in the Everglades, the paper framing her as the "weaker sex" prize-taker. A
commemorative pin records a **200 game at Manor Lanes**. Family photographs
place her beside **Arnold Palmer's private plane**; per the 2026 capture, Dan
extracted the full Palmer history out of her on golf-cart rides across the
course during the caregiving year.

The seat of all of it was **117 Belmont Circle**
([[wiki/places/117-belmont-circle]]), the three-storey house on the edge of the
UCC fairway she held for over fifty years. Directly next door, for those same
five decades, lived [[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie Ulmer's]] grandparents —
the families "super tight for fucking ever." That adjacency is the physical
root of the Uniontown generational loop: Dan's mother dated Annie's father in
high school, and two generations later Dan and Annie's own decade began sixty
feet from where their families' had.

## The patron

Fran ran a documented gift protocol — **$100 per year of the recipient's
life**, from about age 13 ($1,400 at fourteen, $1,500 at fifteen, $2,000 at
twenty). At the hinge of Dan's career she went past it: she bought him a
**Numark NS7 II** controller on top of the annual cheque. The 2026 capture adds
the detail that matters — it was **not spontaneous**. Dan *convinced her* to
buy it. He asked; she paid. The founding act of the entire production identity
is a successful piece of persuasion aimed at a willing patron, which is the
same faculty [[wiki/mind/concepts/acquisition-drive]] documents operating
elsewhere at far worse cost-benefit ratios. The corpus confirms the machine was
in service — an April 2017 message walks someone through installing "SERATO DJ
and driver for numark ns7 II" — though it does not date the gift.

Every alias since (SLOPPP, MOGZART, GRIPNOTIC) and the involuntary sub-bass
signature running through them ([[wiki/interests/music/concepts/sub-bass-signature]])
trace to the patronage of a woman born in a West Virginia coal town in 1920.

The patronage also ran in a smaller, more ordinary register: **paid work at her
house, timed around her Florida winters.** On **2016-01-18** Dan tells Annie he
has *"went to grams and made a list of work to do for her tomorrow"* because he
needs money for a tux; Annie points out the event *"[isn't] until the end of
February,"* and he answers *"I know but she leaves for fla this week."* The
snowbird departure set the deadline — the work had to be done and paid before
she left. It is a small window onto the routine mechanics of the arrangement
that the large gifts obscure, and it independently dates one of the Florida
winters the page otherwise refers to only in general terms.

## The caregiving arrangement (≈October 2017 – April 2018)

This is the chapter the corpus changes most. The page previously described
Dan and Annie as paid round-the-clock caregivers across "~2015–2018." Dan's own
contemporaneous account is narrower and more specific: **six months, full time,
at $15 an hour**, which places the paid arrangement at roughly October 2017
onward and makes the earlier years informal rather than employed. The money
came through the estate and was authorised by its trustee — in March 2018 Dan
reasons that *"fred would have put the kaibosh on me being paid to watch gram
if there was an ongoing issue,"* which puts [[wiki/people/fred-adams|Judge
Adams]] directly in the payroll chain.

The standing directive governing all of it was categorical, and both Dan and
Suz were given it in the same terms: **keep me at home no matter what, do
everything you can to not let me get taken from this house** — with the
rationale everyone understood, that if she entered a facility once she was
never getting out. She turned out to be right, which is why the directive
governs the reading of every decision made in her last year, including some
indefensible ones.

The division of labour was clean and Dan does not flatter himself about it.
Annie was, in his own assessment, "10,000× more responsible" — the changing,
the day-to-day survival, the actual nursing. Dan's contribution was the
opposite and, on the evidence, not the lesser one: he refused to sanitise her
existence and treated a 97-year-old with dementia as a peer. They watched
*Pretty Woman* and analysed it, Fran ruling the prostitute narrative "way
classier than some Cary Grant movie." Already prone to talking to people on the
television when nobody was watching, she was handed something she could not
parse at all — Dan ran *GTA V* in stage mode and detonated several hundred cars
on a photorealistic Los Angeles bridge, producing the line the family kept:
**"WHAT. THE FUCK. IS HAPPENING IN THIS WORLD ANYMORE."** She hit his vape.
There is the photograph of her posing delightedly with his glass bong, which
she had complimented as "a lovely vase," a grey sweatshirt behind her
accidentally composing itself into rising smoke.

He filmed most of that year, which is why any of it survives. His framing of
what he was documenting is the right one: a wild spirit resisting institutional
capture, in the months before the institution got her.

**And the threat was real, not retrospective.** In **November 2017** — four
months before the fall, five before the letters — Dan writes that certain
people must be kept away, *"especially and most specifically not people
directly connected to my grandparents who are looking for any excuse to throw
her in a nursing home."* The family conflict that
[[wiki/people/diane-moore|Diane's]] April 2018 letters formalise was already
understood as an active campaign while Fran was still at home.

## The ending

The fall on the night of **7–8 March 2018** has its own page
([[wiki/timeline/events/the-fall-of-fran]]) because of what else was happening
in the house that night. The medical sequence after it, which the corpus now
dates rather than infers: she **survived surgery** by 9 March; was moved to a
nursing home on or about **Monday 12 March**; deteriorated there exactly as she
had predicted, her mind never returning, looping on a single question — *when
am I leaving?*; was admitted to the Uniontown Hospital pediatric wing on
**1 April**, a room secured by the donations she and Ira had made; was placed
on **hospice around 1–2 April**, at which point staff **stopped feeding her**;
and died on **4 April**, four days later, with Dan and Annie the only two
people in the room. Three minutes later it began to snow. Full account:
[[wiki/timeline/events/fran-death-vigil]].

Dan's reading of the fixation on leaving is that it was about three things at
once — the house, the autonomy, and a specific contempt for absorption into
"the institutional sludge that defined 'normalcy' or pleb existence." It is as
close as the record comes to a statement of her politics, and it is
recognisably the same disposition her great-grandson runs at
[[wiki/mind/synthesis/vertical-authority-skepticism]].

## The death moment, and Dan's retraction of it within 24 hours

The most-quoted passage in this wiki's account of Fran is the moment of death:
Annie asleep at the bedside, Dan awake and alone, silently attempting to
communicate with a woman who had been non-responsive for days — *"if you can
hear me give me some kind of sign"* — and Annie waking him immediately after to
say Fran had blinked for the first time in a week. He went to the bed, told her
he loved her, she smiled, and she died. Writing the next evening he was
explicit that he is "the last person who would be pushing a bullshit story
about something spiritual happening."

**He filmed it, reviewed the footage the same night, and took it back.** At
23:26 on 5 April 2018 — roughly three hours after telling the story — he
messaged his sister:

> "upon further review of the video, it looks like a monitor went off alerting
> us that her pulse was dropping. neither of us remembered it but the video
> helps correct the timeline. def isn't as 'supernatural' knowing that fact.
> but doesn't take away from the cool moment we had with gram."

This is the single most characteristic thing in the record of him. Inside
twenty-four hours of the most emotionally loaded event of his life, holding a
story that had visibly moved everyone he told it to, he went to the primary
source, found the mundane cue that explained the timing — an alarm neither
witness consciously registered — and corrected himself unprompted, to a
family member, at no benefit to himself. The forensic method
([[wiki/mind/concepts/forensic-method]]) is usually documented pointed at
politics, at Annie, at institutions. Here it is pointed at his own most
consoling memory, and it wins.

Both halves belong on the record. The retraction does not delete the moment —
his own final clause refuses that, and the wiki should too.

## The money, and the part that does not flatter him

Fran is the maternal source node of [[wiki/mind/synthesis/estate-money-spine]],
and the corpus documents the anticipation as well as the arrival. In **January
2018**, with her alive and declining, Dan writes to Annie about what comes
"after gram goes and we have a chance to get above water with the
inheritance... i want to seriously get out of here." On **4 April 2018**, the
day she died, he texts an acquaintance: *"inheritance will help with the
mourning proce$$."* Two days later: *"now that i'm not saddled here with gram
anymore....and that inheritance money coming...ya boy is getting out of PA."*
A first cheque arrived **27 April 2018**; he explains the delay on the rest
himself — *"my inheritance doesn't get distributed until we liquidate all her
assets."* The court-ordered distribution of **$144,069.31**, reduced by a
$10,000 advance to a final **$134,069.31**, was released 1 September 2020,
routed through Suz.

These sit alongside genuine grief in the same week, sometimes the same day, and
the page keeps both rather than choosing. The mourning was not performed — the
video, the vigil, the six months, the closure he describes are all real. The
calculation was also not performed. They simply ran concurrently, which is a
more honest description of how inheritance actually works than the reverent
version.

## Why she matters to the architecture

She is the counterweight to the vertical-authority template that governs the
rest of the family record. Structurally the relationship was **lateral**: peer
humour, patronage without control, mutual delight, and an explicit trade she
named herself — she did anything he asked, so he showed up for her.

## Gaps

The nursing home is still unnamed. The NS7 II gift is undated — the corpus
confirms the machine in use by April 2017 but never records its arrival, and
the gift-protocol figures come from operator memory alone. Thomas Whyel's exact
death date within the 1970s is unfixed, as is whether Emmet Van Voorhis died or
the first marriage ended some other way. The **April 2018 cheque** is
unquantified and its relationship to the 2020 distribution unestablished — Dan
calls it "from grandma," which is his usual word for Fran but not exclusively
so. Her nine siblings are named in the GEDCOM and none has a page or appears
anywhere in the message corpus. The claim that she taught Dan to read rests on
two 2026 captures and nothing else; it is plausible and undated. And the
**media ledger remains outstanding**: `IMG_0569.MOV` (the keno-morning lift),
`IMG_4361.MOV` (the last conversation), `IMG_4637.mov`, `1080p.mov` (the
post-death snow) and the death video itself — the one that produced the
retraction above, and therefore the only primary document that could settle
what actually happened in that room — exist only as uploads inside AI sessions.
Locating the originals is the highest-value collection task attached to this
page.
