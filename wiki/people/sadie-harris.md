# Sadie Harris

Sadie Harris (later Sadie Frank) is Dan's paternal great-grandmother and, on
the documentary record, the most immobile person in his family tree. She was
born in Austria on 14 December 1900, arrived in the United States as part of
the Eastern European Jewish immigration, and by 1920 was living in Brownsville,
Fayette County, Pennsylvania — where the census and city-directory record then
holds her, with one brief Uniontown interval, for the next seventy-three years.
She died on 27 November 1997, seventeen days short of her ninety-seventh
birthday, and was buried in Hopwood. Her importance to the wiki is structural
rather than biographical: she is one of the two immigrant anchors of the line
that [[wiki/mind/synthesis/fayette-return]] is a rule about, and her burial
place is part of what sharpened that rule from a county to a cemetery.

is one of continuous Fayette County residence with a single documented move
inside the county and back.

> **CONTRADICTION:** earlier versions of this page and the member table on
> [[wiki/mind/synthesis/fayette-return]] both recorded Sadie as having *died* in
> Hopwood on 27 November 1997. The GEDCOM records no death place at all — only
> the date — and lists Hopwood as the **burial** place. Her last attested
> residence is Brownsville in 1993. The Hopwood association is real but it is a
> grave, not a deathbed, and the distinction matters because the burial-place
> convergence is now doing analytical work the death-place claim was never
> entitled to do.

Her arrival in Fayette County is not independently dated. The first record
placing her there is the 1920 census; her husband [[wiki/people/david-j-frank]]
is documented in Manhattan and the Bronx as late as June 1915, so the couple's
move into the county falls somewhere in that five-year window and is not
narrowed further by anything on disk.

One later record is worth noting without over-reading: a Social Security number
was issued to her in Pennsylvania in **1962**, two years after David's death in
1960. An SSN issued at age sixty-one is consistent either with entering recorded
employment as a widow or with a survivor-benefits claim, and the export does not
say which.

## Why she matters to the Fayette rule

[[wiki/mind/synthesis/fayette-return]] states that no member of the paternal
Frank line who left Fayette County has ended anywhere else. Sadie does not test
that rule by leaving and returning — she never leaves. What she supplies is the
rule's better-supported form, the same service [[wiki/people/rick-frank]]
performs three generations later: the pattern is not *departure implies return*
but *the terminus is always Fayette*, and a seventy-seven-year tenure satisfies
that description more completely than any journey does.

She also supplies the finding that tightened the rule's geography. David died in
Brownsville in 1960 and was buried in Hopwood; Sadie died in 1997 and was buried
in Hopwood; [[wiki/people/morley-frank]] died in Hopwood in 1998 and was buried
in Hopwood. Three burials, spanning thirty-eight years and two generations, in
one small town that none of them was born in and only one of them died in. The
terminus the line orbits is more specific than a county.

Finally, she is half of the parsimony argument that page makes against
[[wiki/mind/synthesis/ancestral-dialectic]]. Both G1 immigrants — Sadie from
Austria, David from Russia — are on the Ashkenazi side. If the return pattern
reproduces across four generations inside that single line, a two-line
psychological dialectic between Ashkenazi hypervigilance and Appalachian
numbness is not required to explain it.

## Gaps

- **No death place.** The GEDCOM date is firm; the place is absent. Whether she
  died in Brownsville, Uniontown, Hopwood or in care elsewhere is unresolved,
  and a death certificate would settle it.
- **Arrival date unknown.** Nothing on disk dates either her emigration from
  Austria or the couple's move from New York City to Fayette County; the 1915
  Bronx and 1920 Brownsville records only bracket it.
- **Nothing personal survives.** This page is entirely residue — census rows,
  directory listings, a burial record. No letters, photographs, anecdotes or
  family stories about her appear anywhere in the corpus, which is itself worth
  recording: the line's matriarch is present in the wiki only as coordinates.
