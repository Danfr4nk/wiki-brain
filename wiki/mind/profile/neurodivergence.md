# Neurodivergence — Autism as Self-Identification

Dan identifies as autistic. The corpus states this plainly and repeatedly —
[[wiki/self/context-core]] carries it in the same breath as his date of birth
and his politics — but the identification traces to no clinical assessment,
no diagnostic date, and no process anywhere in the record. It rests entirely
on his own self-recognition, reinforced by three independent AI-authored
"operating manual" documents that each restate it as a fact already
established rather than argue for it. That is not a reason to discount it —
self-identification is how most late-diagnosed autistic adults arrive at the
label, and the corpus's own typology cluster ([[wiki/mind/profile/index]])
converges on the same measured profile from four unrelated instruments — but
it is the honest shape of the evidence, and no page in this wiki should cite
the label more confidently than that shape supports.

## Where the claim comes from

Three separate documents in `raw/self/dox-md/` — `operating_manual.md`,
`THE_DAN_FRANK_MANUAL.md`, and `THE_DAN_FRANK_BOOTLOADER.md` — each open with
an identity summary that includes "self-identified autistic" as a fixed
descriptor, alongside his age, hometown, and cognitive type. None of the
three explains how the identification was reached; each treats it as
background the reader is assumed to already know, which means each is
repeating a claim rather than independently verifying one. `operating_manual.md`
goes furthest, naming an "Archetype" directly: **"The 'Autistic Truth Seeker'
in the Basement. Prophet-level insight throttled by dysregulated drive
states."** `THE_DAN_FRANK_MANUAL.md` frames it as infrastructure rather than
diagnosis, under a section header that states the interpretive stance the
whole corpus takes: **"Autism as processing layer, not as label."**

This is worth naming as a pattern in its own right: a self-identification
made once, early, gets carried forward unexamined by every subsequent
AI-collaborative session, because each new session is built by feeding the
model the prior documents as ground truth. Three sources repeating the same
line is not three independent confirmations — it is very likely one claim,
copied forward three times. [[wiki/mind/concepts/exocortex]] documents this
mechanism generally (bootloaders that carry forward whatever the last one
asserted); the autism claim is a specific instance of it, and it is exactly
the kind of thing `EXTRACTION_SPEC.md`'s source-tier rule exists to flag: AI-secondary material is not evidence for its own factual assertions, only for
what Dan himself said inside the session.

## In his own words

The clearest primary-source statement in the corpus is not a description but
a question. In an undated Claude conversation transcript (`raw/self/dox-md/Breaking
the anxiety avoidance cycle.md`), Dan asks directly whether the label is
doing real work or excusing him from ordinary standards:

> "is it a pathology or is it legitimate to say that the advice and
> perspective most people would need to receive is not applicable to someone
> as edge case autist wei[r]do as I am"

That is Dan's own framing of the tension the rest of this page holds: the
self-identification is load-bearing enough that he uses it to reason about
which advice applies to him, while simultaneously being uncertain, in his own
words, whether that move is legitimate self-knowledge or a rationalization.
No page in the corpus resolves that tension for him, and this one does not
either — it is recorded as an open question in his own voice rather than
smoothed into a settled conclusion on either side.

## The behavioral texture `operating_manual.md` attributes to it

Independent of the diagnostic question, `operating_manual.md` catalogs a set
of specific, dated behavioral patterns it groups under the "Autistic Truth
Seeker" archetype — these are behavior-level claims, checkable against the
message record rather than typological inference, and are kept separate here
from the label itself:

- **Information intake at unsustainable volume.** Political, market, and
  conspiracy content consumed at a rate the document calls "18 hours a day"
  territory; Dan's own self-aware line to a friend: *"Fortunately for you,
  you have a family and a life and actual adult stuff that keeps you from
  spending 18 hours a day reading and following and shit."*
- **Spectacle over outcome.** His stated relationship to news and politics is
  aesthetic first: *"I can't tell you how much fun I'm having with it,"* and
  a preference, stated outright, for "the timeline with maximum chaos"
  regardless of which side is winning.
- **The evangelist compulsion.** A five-plus-year pattern — discover a tool,
  demo it, share it, then register frustration that nobody else cares as
  much — applied across a long, heterogeneous list the document names
  explicitly: PimEyes, Anna's Archive, NotebookLM, iPhone Personal Voice,
  Google AI Studio, Veo 3, stock tips, audiobooks, political analysis.
- **Brevity guilt without brevity.** Explicit self-awareness of being a
  "multi-message texter" who sends long messages, including an apology to a
  friend (Jerad) for "making you suffer with 5000-word messages" — paired
  with the document's own note that the behavior continues regardless of the
  awareness.

Each of these is a claim about documented behavior rather than about
etiology, and each is independently checkable against the message corpus in
a way the identity label itself is not — a natural next pass for a session
with time to re-derive intake rates and message-length distributions the way
[[wiki/mind/profile/texting-deviance-audit]] already does for turn-level
cadence.

## Convergence with the rest of the typology stack

No instrument in [[wiki/mind/profile/index]] was designed to test for autism,
and none of them cite the autism claim or each other. That makes their
agreement more interesting than if they had been built to agree:
[[wiki/mind/profile/intp]]'s Ti-dominant, Fe-weak read; the deviance audit's
97/100 linguistic-style outlier and 90/100 social-aversion outlier
([[wiki/mind/profile/deviance-mapping]]); and the stylometric profile's
23,286-word casual-texting vocabulary and 2,465-instance "because" count
([[wiki/mind/profile/linguistic-profile]]) all describe, in their own
vocabulary, the same shape: language and logic used as a processing
mechanism rather than a social one, and a systematizing style that runs
independent of whether anyone else is following it. That convergence is
weak evidence for the underlying trait being real and measurable; it is not
evidence that "autism" specifically, as opposed to some other explanation for
the same shape, is the correct name for it — the corpus has no instrument
capable of distinguishing between those two possibilities.

## Gaps

- **No clinical or diagnostic record exists anywhere in `raw/`.** There is no
  date of self-identification, no account of what prompted it, and no
  mention of a formal evaluation ever being sought. This is answerable by
  Dan directly in one paragraph — when the self-identification happened and
  what specifically prompted it — and would let this page distinguish a
  long-held self-understanding from something adopted more recently, inside
  the AI-collaborative sessions that now repeat it as fact.
- **No sensory, motor, or childhood-developmental evidence has been located.**
  The typical corroborating detail for a late self-identification — sensory
  sensitivities, special interests with the specific intensity/narrowness
  signature, childhood social difficulty independent of the adult record —
  has not turned up in a targeted search of the corpus. Absence noted rather
  than assumed resolved: a fuller pass through `raw/self/dox-scan/gmail_bodies.txt`
  (the least-mined source in the corpus, per `BACKLOG.md`) or a direct
  question to Dan would settle it.
- **No comorbidity discussion.** ADHD, commonly co-occurring with autism in
  adult self-identification, is not raised anywhere in the sources reviewed
  for this page. Not evidenced either way.
- **Whether the label carries any practical consequence — disclosure,
  accommodation, treatment-seeking — is undocumented.** The corpus frames it
  purely as an interpretive lens on his own cognition, never as something
  that changed a decision.
