# Twitter / X — 2024

**Peak volume, and the densest single day in seventeen years.** 258 originals,
and 23 of them land on **28 June**, the night of the first Biden–Trump debate.
`wiki/mind/synthesis/twitter-2024-cognitive-state` reads this year as
adversarial reality-testing disguised as political posting; this page carries
the material that reading is built on, plus three things it does not: the
debate night as a real-time record of a prediction failing, the Roman history
functioning as a working analytical frame, and the fact that he was a caddie.

## 28 June: a forecast collapsing, minute by minute, in public

He posted predictions **before** the debate:

> *"Against my better judgement, here are predictions for tonight's
> #Debates2024 1. Biden is going to outperform the comically low expectations
> that his critics have set…"*

Then the debate started:

> *"LETS FUCKING GOOOOOOO"* → *"This is not SOTU Joe"* → *"Oh my god his
> voice"* → *"It's joever"* → *"This is catastrophic"* → *"It's hard to
> overstate just how devastating this is for the Biden campaign."*

**That is a public, timestamped record of a stated prediction being falsified in
front of the person who made it, and of him updating without pause or
defence.** He does not delete the prediction, argue that it was nearly right, or
reframe the evidence. Within an hour he is running the consequences — betting
markets, Morning Joe as a leading indicator, three New York Times podcasts
about replacing the candidate — and by 6 July he publishes a numbered *"list of
facts about the facts on the ground as of 3:15am"* with an explicit probability
attached: any replacement is *"25% at best"*.

`wiki/meta/testimony-veracity` scores what Dan claims. This night is the
clearest specimen in the corpus of how he behaves when a claim of his is
disproved immediately and publicly, and the answer is: he says so and moves on
in the same hour. Set beside 2023's *"Fooled AGAIN by the fucking liberal
mirage"*, the pattern holds across at least two years.

## The Roman republic is a working instrument, not an interest

`wiki/interests/roman-republic` records the 2024 reading binge and that it
started on YouTube — *"After I finished the @HistoriaCivilis Caesar/Octavian
series, I started devouring the 'essential' books about the late Roman
Republic"* (8 April), Dan Carlin's *Death Throes* praised for *"the
proto-Marxian narrative that underlies the whole era"* (9 April).

What this page adds is that **within three weeks he is using it to read current
events**, unprompted and without explanation:

- **1 May**, on the UCLA encampment: *"appears to be utilizing the Fabian strategy and I respect those kind of Caesarian tactics."*
- **28 June**, on the post-debate left: *"when we do global communism and start up the proscription list, I'm using my first pick on Jill Biden"* — a proscription list being the Sullan and triumviral instrument he had just spent two months reading about.
- **5 June**, correcting a major publication: *"Writing an article for a major publication about republican Rome's second Civil War without knowing the difference between Iler…"*

That is the *"historicize"* step
`wiki/mind/synthesis/twitter-2024-cognitive-state` names, caught in the act and
sourced: the history is not a hobby running beside the politics, it is the
vocabulary he reaches for when the politics get strange. And the direction of
supply is documented — a YouTube series, then nine books, then the frame.

## He was a caddie

**28 June**, inside an argument about Trump's golf handicap:

> *"I've spent a lot of time on golf courses with all sorts of golfers during
> my time as a caddie."*

That is the fourth job to enter this archive the same way — as the credential
for an aside, never as a fact stated. The 2016 page named the pattern (the job
as material, never as subject) and it now spans retail (2012), an unpaid
internship (2011), waiting tables (2022, 2023) and caddying, with no start
dates, no employers and no duration for any of them.

It also completes the golf thread. `wiki/self/twitter/2016` has him at the U.S.
Open at Oakmont and reaching for *"foreclosure"* as the simile for the rough;
2009 has *"country club status. bloated sense of self worth"*; 2014 has the
great-grandmother as *"the country club Queen Bee in 1960"*. He was inside the
country club, carrying the bags.

## Where the politics go

The year's arc is the Biden campaign's collapse, and Dan's position moves with
it in a direction worth recording. He begins in February as a worried tactician
— an electoral-college thread with Georgia and Arizona polling and historical
voting attached (14 February). By 29 June he is threatening exit: *"I'll be
changing my registration to independent if the Democratic Party doesn't move to
either persuade or force Biden to make a fundamental change to the ticket."* By
30 June he is reconsidering the premise entirely: *"now I'm starting to think
@briebriejoy might have had a point about electoralism after all."*

**That is the departure mechanism again**, the one running through the
Republican exit of 2009, the neo-atheism exit of 2013 and the O&A exit somewhere
before 2018 — the position becomes untenable because of the people holding it
(*"The 'keep Biden' squad are truly some of the most vile and braindead people
on planet earth"*, 3 July), and the exit is threatened before any alternative
is named. What is different in 2024 is that he says it out loud while it is
happening, which none of the earlier ones did.

## Texture

*"2024 year of the dragon"* posted, alone, roughly a dozen times across the
year, with no explanation offered on any of them. A great-grandfather taken to
a rated-R film by a ten-year-old who did not know what the rating meant
(1 April). Sustained criticism of the Israeli campaign in Gaza, including
26 March, and a note on rhetoric so bad *"that both sides of the
Israel-Palestine divide can find common ground in their reactions of shared
disgust"* (5 April). And on 2 May, a small demonstration of the media instinct:
he notices a local affiliate cutting its helicopter feed of the UCLA protest,
predicts police will move within ten minutes, and reports back — *"Lmao it was
exactly 10 mins."*

## What would change this reading

The debate-night reading treats a stream of reactions as evidence of
calibration behaviour. An alternative reading is that it is simply a man
watching television and reacting, and the prediction was too casual to count as
one; what argues against that is that he framed it as a prediction himself
(*"here are predictions for tonight's"*) and never returned to defend it. The
caddie fact is one clause with no date — it could refer to adolescence rather
than adult employment, and nothing here settles which.
