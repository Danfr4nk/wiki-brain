# YouTube Watch History (2007–2026)

**The short version.** Dan's YouTube history goes back nearly twenty years and is one of the most detailed records of his attention that exists. It shows a loud early stretch built almost entirely around archived shock-radio shows, a long quiet middle, and a big return in 2022 built around left-wing political commentary. But the headline numbers are not what they look like. A large share of the logged "watches" are advertisements, not videos he chose. And the whole export only covers one of his several YouTube accounts. **Every count here is a floor, not a total.**

## Two things that break the raw numbers

Anyone reading this record should know these before reading any figure in it.

**First: a lot of the "watches" are ads.** The original export logged 17,302 "Watched" events. When someone actually checked which of those were real videos and which were served advertisements, **14.1% of the 15,149 entries turned out to be Google Ads.** That puts the real count of videos he chose to watch closer to **13,011**. None of the per-year tables in the full entry were ever filtered this way, so they are an upper bound on real engagement, not the engagement itself.

**Second, and larger: this is one account out of several.** The Google login has multiple YouTube accounts attached. That means every number in this record — old export and new — describes only what happened on this one account. Dan has confirmed that his real total viewing across all of them is higher than any figure here. Until somebody produces a combined export covering all the accounts, the honest reading is that **these are lower bounds, not a census.**

That second point is not a footnote. It arrived as a correction to a finding this record had already drawn, and it changed what the finding meant.

## The gap that looked like a collapse and wasn't

Splitting real videos from ad impressions turned up something dramatic: between **November 8, 2025 and February 15, 2026** — about 100 days — this account records exactly **one** real video watched. That was "Man in Cave (Reupload)", on November 8 itself.

Everything else logged in that window was an advertisement. **78 of them**, often in dense back-to-back runs with no real videos in between — roughly 40 consecutive ads on January 4, 2026 across three and a half hours, another cluster of about 15 on December 26, 2025.

That looked like a person who had stopped watching anything. And it sat exactly on top of one of the most intense documented stretches in his personal life, which made it read like evidence of a shutdown.

**It wasn't.** Dan confirmed he was almost certainly watching on a different one of his YouTube accounts during that window; this export simply does not cover it. The dense ad-only clusters fit that perfectly — a device still logged in, passively being served ads, while the real watching happened somewhere the export cannot see.

This is worth sitting with, because it is the clearest example of how the whole record can mislead. A hundred days of near-silence in a behavioural log is a striking finding. It was an artifact of which account got exported.

## The other thing that looks like a spike and isn't

The most recent months appear to show an enormous surge in activity — 1,036 logged events in June 2026, and 1,616 in just the first twenty days of July.

Filter the ads out and it mostly disappears. **80.3%** of June's entries and **96.4%** of July's are advertisements. Real videos watched: **204** in June and only **58** in the first twenty days of July.

## What he actually watched, in three eras

**2011–2013: the radio archive years.** These are the two loudest years in the entire log — 3,538 logged events in 2012, 2,742 in 2013 — and they are not scattered browsing. They are substantially one subject: fan-uploaded archives of the Opie & Anthony shock-radio universe. Three separate fan-archive channels sit near the top of the all-time list. That is the signature of somebody working through a back catalogue until it runs out, not returning to a favourite.

**2014–2021: the long quiet.** Volume drops hard and stays down — 708 in 2014, 353 in 2015, 122 in 2016, and a low of **78 in 2019**. There is a small bump in 2020 around the election (295), and Last Week Tonight enters the picture. 2021, during a difficult employment stretch, sits at 158.

**2022–present: politics.** The return is sharp and it has a clear shape: **2,256** logged events in 2022 and **2,622** in 2023. The channels are left political commentary — Vaush (177 watches in 2023 alone), The Majority Report, David Pakman, Breaking Points, HasanAbi, Some More News — alongside civics and history from Mr. Beat (98 in 2023). Trump appears in **301** video titles; "election" in 61. Music also moves onto YouTube's dedicated music service in this period, around 2,370 watches, peaking at 987 in 2025.

The most recent year, counting real watches only, keeps that spine: Breaking Points 112, Last Week Tonight 95, The Majority Report 93, plus names new to this record — Tor's Cabinet of Curiosities (91), We're In Hell (42), JREG (23), STRANGE ÆONS (22), exurb1a (19). There is also a late-night cluster of 2000s pop-punk nostalgia on June 30, 2026: Green Day and Fall Out Boy live sessions.

Across the whole log there are **4,146** unique channels — wide breadth alongside a handful of very deep niches.

## What this record is good for

Used carefully, it is an unusually honest witness, because he was not writing it down — it accumulated whether or not he was paying attention to it. It tracks the shift in where his information came from, from pre-social-media radio archives through the 2020s explosion of online political video. It lines up with other records of the same years: his Facebook likes from 2012 to 2014 match the radio-and-electronic-music era exactly.

One thing it can date but not explain: the 2022-onward political viewing is the downstream trace of a political shift the message record dates precisely to August 2020. The watch history shows that the turn **stuck**. It does not show where it came from.

## What we still don't know

- **The big one: there is no combined export.** Every count here covers a single account. Until all of the accounts are exported together, no figure on this record can be treated as a total — including the daily averages other entries have quoted from it.

- **The old per-year tables were never ad-filtered.** Every year-by-year number from the original export mixes real videos with ad impressions. Only the most recent stretch has been properly separated.

- **240 entries near the old export's cutoff vanished from the new one.** Every sampled example was an ad impression from the same three-day boundary window, so this looks like Google's ad logging simply not being identical run to run. It affects a handful of ad rows, not real viewing data.
