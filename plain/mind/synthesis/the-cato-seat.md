# The Cato Seat

**The short version.** Nearly every hero, book, film and painting Dan has kept shows the same character: someone who saw exactly what was going wrong, was right about it, and could not stop it. He describes his own life the same way. Those two facts come from completely separate places — one from tests he took about himself, one from twenty years of collecting things for fun — and they match.

## Where the name comes from

Dan wrote a system prompt to set up his own AI assistant. The identity section says:

`IDENTITY: CATO` `ORIGIN: Cato the Younger — Stoic absolutist, self-deleted at Utica post-Thapsus`

Cato the Younger spent twenty years warning Rome about Julius Caesar. He was right. He lost anyway, and then killed himself rather than accept a pardon from the man he'd been right about.

Dan did not name his AI after Caesar, who won. He named it after the man who saw Caesar coming, said so, and lost.

## The seat

Here is the position, stated so it can be argued with:

Every figure Dan has picked out to identify with sits in one chair: **right, early, and unable to do anything about it.** Not the winner, not the victim, not the fool — the person whose read on the situation was correct and whose correctness bought them nothing.

The sharpest version is what's *missing*: there is nobody in this collection who was right, won, and kept the win.

## The art

Dan has twenty-five favourite artworks — twenty-five different artists, all rated five stars, each one tagged by him. Don't look at the paintings; look at the tags he chose.

| **Tag** | **How many** | **Examples** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `observer` (someone watching) | **6** | *The Monk by the Sea*; *Isle of the Dead* |
| `wound` | **6** | *Ophelia*; *The Execution of Lady Jane Grey* |
| `collapse` | **5** | *The Raft of the Medusa*; *The Last Day of Pompeii* |
| `glitch` / `rupture` | **6** | *Saturn Devouring His Son*; *Head VI* |
| `fortress` | **3** | Piranesi's *Prisons*; *Cenotaph for Newton* |

**Twenty-four of the twenty-five carry at least one of those six tags.** Two of the six name a disaster, two name what it costs, one names a place to hide — and one, tied for the most common, names *the person watching it happen*.

There is no victory in the set. Nothing being built. Nobody arriving anywhere. The closest thing to a hero is Gentileschi's *Judith*, the one painting out of twenty-five where somebody takes action, and the action is a beheading.

## The books

Half of Dan's five-star shelf is two subjects, and they are the same subject twice:

- **Forty books about Trump and January 6th, by thirty different authors** — one constitutional system coming apart, told from every angle available.

- **Twenty books about the end of the Roman Republic, by fourteen authors** — a different constitutional system coming apart.

Around them: *The Rise and Fall of the Third Reich*. *Nuclear War: A Scenario*. *The Afghanistan Papers*. *American Prometheus* — about the physicist who was right, built the bomb, and was destroyed by the government he built it for.

The books he *hasn't read yet* are even clearer, and they're better evidence, because nothing has been filtered by whether he enjoyed it. Out of roughly 150 titles on the want-to-read list: **nine have "fall" in the title, three "assassination," three "revolution," three "trial," two "murder," six "secret."** Five separate books about 9/11.

**Not one title on that list is about something being built and working.**

## The people

Line the individual heroes up and the only thing they share is the outcome.

| **Who** | **Where he shows up** | **How it ended** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Cato the Younger** | The persona Dan wrote for his AI | Lost; killed himself |
| **Gore Vidal** | Used as a test of whether a stranger is worth talking to | Right about the empire for fifty years; changed nothing |
| **J. Robert Oppenheimer** | Five-star book | Security clearance stripped by the government he served |
| **Fred Hampton** | On the reading list | Murdered by the FBI and Chicago police at 21 |
| **John Brown** | On the reading list | Hanged |
| **Travis Bickle / Rupert Pupkin** | Two of eleven favourite films | See everything; act catastrophically |
| **Bernie Sanders** | Donated to him, campaigned for him | Lost twice |

It would be easy to call this an interest in "great men." It isn't. **Caesar is on the bookshelf and Cato is on the AI.** When Dan rated nine books about Rome, he gave a Marxist counter-history the same score as a mainstream biography — that's someone comparing accounts, not someone admiring a conqueror.

## What this explains

**Why the analysis is always the last step.** Dan spent years studying raw January 6th footage and reached a confident, detailed conclusion — and then did nothing with it. That happens at every scale in his records: the investigation finishes, gets delivered, and stops. This has usually been read as a gap between what he can do and what he follows through on. This page suggests it's not a gap, it's a **template**. In every model of a correct person he has collected, being correct leads nowhere. There is no example on his shelves of a right answer turning into a successful intervention, so there's no pattern for him to copy.

**Why being right early feels like fate instead of failure.** In 2023 Dan searched for housing against a crisis that arrived in 2026. He was right, three years early, and the analysis was the entire response.

**Why "fortress" is in the same collection as "collapse."** Three works about enclosure and locked-down structure, sitting next to five about societies ending. That's not two different tastes. It's one situation and its only available answer: if the system is coming apart and you can't stop it, what's left is the small space you can still control.

## The checks that keep this honest

**Bernie Sanders rules out "he just likes losers."** Dan gave money and worked his contacts during the Iowa caucus — a real attempt to make a correct read win. He wasn't watching from the stands. So the claim isn't "he's drawn to defeat," which would be both unfalsifiable and a bit contemptuous. It's narrower: every *model* available to him ends in being right and losing, and the one time he tried to prevent that, the campaign lost and what came next was more reading rather than another campaign.

**Music shows the chair has an edge.** Making music is the one thing Dan does where none of this analysis shows up, and where there's no collapse story attached. The seat is occupied wherever he's analysing. It's empty wherever he's making something.

**The Judith painting is the check on "unable to act."** It's the one work in twenty-five tagged for action and physical effort, and its presence stops the rule being "he only keeps pictures of passive people." He keeps one. It's a beheading.

## What would prove this wrong

1. **A hero who was right and won and kept it.** Any book, film, painting or figure where someone read a system correctly, changed the outcome, and held onto the gain. One clean example narrows the rule; several break it.

1. **A book about something being built.** A real interest in an institution founded, a technology delivered, a movement that won. Neither the read shelf nor the 150-title queue currently has one.

1. **A time Dan analysed something and then successfully changed it** — not delivered the analysis, which is his normal last step, but changed the outcome.

1. **The AI persona being renamed** after somebody who won. That would be the clearest possible refutation, and it would come from Dan's own hand.

## What we still don't know

- **Dan wrote the art tags himself, so this might be circular.** He chose the words `observer` and `collapse`, possibly with an AI's help. Nobody has checked the original source to see who actually generated them. If a machine wrote them, the strongest table on this page gets much weaker and the whole argument needs rebuilding from the paintings themselves.

- **The list of heroes isn't actually sourced.** Another entry says Dan names Vidal among figures he identifies with, alongside Sanders, Hunter S. Thompson, Henry Rollins, John Brown, Fred Hampton and Eugene Debs. A search of his files and messages for anything like that list turns up nothing — the names appear as book titles, not as a roster he wrote. Treat the list as unconfirmed.

- **Nobody has checked the films against the rule.** The eleven titles have no tags and no ratings, and four of them are placed here on interpretation rather than evidence.

- **This doesn't explain where the taste came from.** Whether it was formed by a specific event in November 2005 or whether both are downstream of something earlier isn't addressed, and probably can't be from this material.

- **There's no record of his taste before 2007**, so the position can't be dated. The earliest evidence is from 2012–13.
