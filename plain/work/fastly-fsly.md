# Fastly (FSLY)

**The short version.** Fastly is the stock that opened the door. Not a company Dan worked for, not a person he knew — a ticker symbol, FSLY, that a childhood friend texted him about on September 20, 2020. That tip converted Dan from a spectator into an active retail trader, and the trade itself taught him the lesson he would carry through the next year of meme-stock chaos: momentum matters more than conviction. He got in near the top of a vertical run, skimmed a quick profit, and got out before the crash. The advice he'd been given — "buy and don't touch for 2-3 years" — would have meant buying the peak and eating the collapse. Instead, he caught the exact window between the all-time-high candles and the earnings blowup, and the lesson he took from the dip shaped every trade that followed.

## The setting — September 2020

The tip landed in a specific moment. The pandemic had been raging for six months. The country was in what Dan's own notes call a "micro-radius siege" — movement restricted, attention compressed to the screen in front of you, the outside world reduced to a set of digital feeds. The stock market had crashed in March and then done something that surprised everyone: it came roaring back. Retail trading exploded. Stimulus checks went into brokerage accounts. People stuck at home started watching tickers the way they'd once sports scores. The conditions were perfect for a tip to land hard: attention had nowhere else to go, and the barrier to buying a stock was an app on your phone.

Dan was not a trader before this. He was a spectator — someone who noticed patterns, who had a habit of reading cultural signals in politics, music, relationships. The pandemic compressed all of that pattern-recognition energy into a single new channel: the stock market. Fastly was the first real bet he placed.

## The tip

The entry point was a text from a childhood friend, Jerad Friedline, not Dan's own research. The messages are preserved verbatim in the record.

On September 20, 2020, at 7:40 PM, Jerad wrote: "FSLY... busy at the moment but will send my research tomorrow. I made a little over $200k from April — now from this stock alone, future of web hosting and edge CDN."

Then: "Amazon is using Fastly on Amazon.com over their own AWS hosting, that's all you need to know."

And the line that started it: "Buy FSLY and do not touch for 2-3 years."

Dan's response came twenty-three minutes later: "just buy FSLY?"

Jerad confirmed: "Buy FSLY and do not touch for 2-3 years."

Dan bought in. By September 30 he told Jerad "112 shares my dude" and "fastly killing it." On October 6: "dude i made $600 just from FSLY today." He had dumped roughly $15k of his portfolio into the position and taken a quick profit of about $4k before the meme-stock eruption widened the game in January 2021.

## Why the timing was lucky, not just the pick

The entry landed in the steepest part of the run, not early. FSLY had already climbed from about $15 in April to about $30 by mid-September — on the back of Jerad's own "$200k from April" gains. Dan bought in late September and rode the vertical tail. On October 7, Jerad noted "Didn't see it breaking all time high this quick," the same day Fastly announced a Google Cloud partnership and the narrative that TikTok was hosting on Fastly (TikTok had just passed Instagram in millennial users) was peaking. Dan skimmed a $600 day on October 6 and was out before the exit.

The trap in Jerad's "don't touch for 2-3 years" advice is visible in hindsight. Fastly's late-2020 earnings report guided margins down on TikTok uncertainty, and the stock dropped sharply in a single session — one of the worst large-cap drops of that earnings season. Dan's later messages are him watching the aftermath, not the ascent: "FSLY earnings tomorrow" on February 17, 2021, and the February 4 reflection below. He caught the exact window between the all-time-high candles and the guidance blowup. The entry was good luck on timing as much as a good pick.

## The lesson — the big dip

Fastly is where the era's central thesis got burned in. The stock's sharp earnings-driven drop in late 2020 is what Dan read as a momentum lesson rather than a buy-the-dip opportunity. His own words, from a message sent on February 4, 2021:

"I learned a lot from that big fastly dip about the importance of momentum trading. I never get FOMO about stuff I've sold that has gains later, but you feel it hard when you miss a good opportunity to get out."

He was still watching it into 2021: "FSLY earnings tomorrow" on February 17. The takeaway — sell strength, don't marry the thesis — is the same market-era insight that Dan's own operating manual states plainly: cultural-trend sensing had overtaken fundamentals, and the skill was reading the crowd, not the balance sheet.

## Why it matters

Fastly is the hinge of the market era: the one tip that converted a pattern-recognition habit — normally spent on politics, music, relationships — onto retail markets during the pandemic's "micro-radius siege." The lucky part is the precision of the timing: in near the top of the vertical run, out before the late-2020 earnings crash. "Buy and don't touch for 2-3 years" would have meant buying the top and eating the collapse. Instead Dan skimmed the ascent and learned momentum from the dip.

It is also the cleanest instance of the evangelism pattern. Jerad passed the tip to Dan. Dan passed it onward to another friend who "started calling in daily gains," matching the relational-broadcast instinct that runs through Dan's social world. The position's modest size — about $15k in, about $4k profit, a single $600 day — is the floor under the later, larger swings in GME, AMC, and CCIV that would define the meme-stock eruption.

## The larger pattern

Fastly was the first trade, but it was not the last. The lesson Dan took from the big dip — momentum over conviction, sell strength, don't marry the thesis — became the operating principle for the larger, more chaotic trades that followed in early 2021. The meme-stock eruption, the GME and AMC squeezes, the CCIV SPAC saga — all of those came after Fastly, and all of them ran on the same engine: reading the crowd, not the balance sheet. Fastly taught Dan that the crowd could be read, that the momentum could be ridden, and that the exit mattered more than the thesis. The $15k position and the $4k profit were the proof of concept. Everything that followed was the scaling up.

The position size is worth pausing over. $15k was not a trivial sum for Dan in 2020 — it was a serious commitment, the kind of money that hurts when it goes wrong. And the $4k profit he took was real money, not a paper gain. It was enough to prove the concept without being enough to change his life. That modesty is part of what makes Fastly the cleanest case study: the stakes were high enough to matter, low enough to walk away from. The later trades would not have that property. By the time GME hit, the stakes were life-changing, and the exit discipline that Fastly had taught him would be tested in a completely different way.
