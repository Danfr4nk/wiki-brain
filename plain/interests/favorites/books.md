# Book Favorites

**The short version.** Dan has rated 120 books across 98 different authors. On its face that looks like impressive breadth. Count the books by subject instead of by author, and the reading list collapses into two obsessions carried by forty-four hands: the last decade of American politics, and the last century of the Roman Republic. The 98-author spread isn't a measure of range. It's what monomania looks like when it reads everything available on a single subject.

## The two subjects, counted by author

The contradiction changes how to read the 98-author figure.

- **Forty books tagged ****`trump`**** or ****`jan-6`****, by thirty authors** — the four-year degradation of a constitutional order, reconstructed from every available vantage point. *Fire and Fury*, *Siege*, *Landslide*, *Betrayal*, *The Divider*, *Confidence Man*.

- **Twenty books tagged ****`roman-republic`****, ****`ancient-history`****, or ****`caesar`****, by fourteen authors** — the last generation of a different constitutional order. *Death Throes of the Republic*, *Rubicon*, *The Assassination of Julius Caesar*.

The two sets do not overlap. Exactly half the collection is two subjects read through forty-four different hands. Eighty-five of the 98 authors appear exactly once — 86.7% — because one account per witness is all that exhaustive coverage of a single event requires. The author-level spread is produced by subject-level monomania, not by breadth.
