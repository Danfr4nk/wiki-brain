# Food and Diet

**The short version.** Food is everywhere in Dan's record — as work, as damage, as region, as metaphor, even as permanent body art. What it never is, anywhere in the archive, is something he has a taste opinion about. There are no favourite foods, no cuisine preferences, no better-or-worse rankings. Instead there is a composition rule, and it is total: a food may contain only what he specified in advance, and anything that permeates the dish — onion, tomato, pickle — is refused outright before the plate arrives. That rule is not pickiness. It is the same provenance filter the rest of this life runs on, executed on a plate, in under a second, without an argument.

## The finding, stated plainly

Food appears in this corpus in every register except one. It is labour — three documented jobs, one of them a forty-one-month immersion in a single cuisine. It is pathology — an adolescent eating disorder, candy as a staple, two teeth gone. It is region, in the form of a funeral cookie retold for thirty years. It is metaphor, in "information diet" and in the instruction that "the food and the cat are always real." It is even a tattoo on his forearm. **What it never is is something he has judged.**

That absence is not an absence of contact. Food is talked about more than music is in the same message archive — and music produces 1,860 curated verdicts while food produces none. The domain is not under-observed. It is unjudged.

## The regime: subtraction as an absolute policy

The staples themselves are not the finding. Dan eats, by his own account, "quite literally, like a toddler" — chicken strips, french fries, candy and fruit as staples. The finding is the rule applied to everything else, which he states is "an absolute, dealbreaking, no-questions-asked policy":

| **Item** | **Permitted composition** |
| --- | --- |
| Cheeseburger | meat, cheese, bread |
| Taco | meat, cheese, shell |
| Sandwich | meat, cheese, roll |
| Vegetables accepted | corn, lettuce — and no others |
| Refused outright | anything containing onion or tomato |
| Absolute exclusion | pickles, "the mortal enemy" |

Read the middle column again. Every permitted item is a named food **with its additions removed**, reduced to the constituents he specified. Nothing is ranked against anything else. What the regime governs is not which foods are good but **what is allowed to be in them** — and the answer is: only what was asked for.

The refusal list is not random either. Corn and lettuce are separable and inert; they sit alongside the rest of the plate without changing it. Onion, tomato and pickle are the three that **permeate** — they wet the bread, they flavour everything they touch, and they cannot be picked out after the fact. The accepted vegetables are the ones that can be removed. The refused ones are the ones that have already committed the food to something before it reaches him.

## This is the provenance filter in its most literal form

The rest of this life runs on a single question: *was this handed to me, or did I select it?* Food appeared at first to be the one high-contact domain that question never entered. That reading was wrong. **Food is not exempt from the sort. It is where the sort is enforced most strictly and most physically.** A sandwich that arrives with something on it that he did not specify is an object containing an unrequested component, and it is refused outright rather than negotiated — the same operation the rest of the corpus performs on received culture and unverified claims from authority, executed on a plate, in under a second, without an argument.

The distinction the earlier reading missed is between **evaluation** and **specification**. He does not judge food. He specifies it, and enforcement is absolute. Those are different faculties, and only the first one leaves a favourites list.

## Why the rule is all-or-nothing

The most useful thing about the food rule is not how strict it is. It is that the strictness has a known cause, and the cause is the same one that explains several other things about Dan.

His mind runs a pass/fail test. It asks whether a thing holds together, not how much of it holds together. There is no second setting that produces a verdict like *"fine, in a small amount."* The part of a person that would normally make that softer call — the part that weighs how others feel and lands somewhere in the middle — is the weakest part of his profile by a wide margin.

So a sandwich that turns up with something on it he did not ask for does not fail by degrees. It fails. There is no room to talk him into "close enough," because the function that would run that negotiation is barely there.

That is the same account the rest of the wiki gives for much bigger things: worth, taste, trust, and his frozen verdict on a favourite band. Here it runs on a plate instead of a person, and that is what makes food the clearest case of all. A person can be argued with, flattered, or given the benefit of the doubt. A sandwich cannot. So the on/off switch shows with nothing to blur it.

One honest caveat: the profile scores behind this explanation were commissioned by Dan about himself, not measured against his message record. The composition rule itself comes straight from him and is solid. The explanation for *why* it is absolute is the best account available, not an independently checked fact.

## The five registers food does occupy

**Labour.** Au Za'atar (2021–2024) is the strongest control available: forty-one months as busser and server inside a Midtown Lebanese restaurant, daily exposure to tableside shawarma and specific wine varietals, and what the corpus records is affection for the Lebanese cultural tradition and the humour — plus the night the owners comped his mother's entire menu. The culture registered. The cuisine did not.

Caviar Courier (2019–2020) is food as freight: 290 messages across a year, every one an automated dispatch notification, nothing about what was in the bag. BFS Foods (2026) is food as counter labour at the return pole of the geography — gas-station food and cigarettes dispensed, not chosen.

**Pathology.** The only material that treats food as a subject in its own right is entirely about damage: "bulimic as a high schooler," candy as a staple of the diet, and the adult dental consequence of two lost teeth, one a visible canine. Food enters the body record as harm and leaves it again.

**Region.** The Eat'n Park *Frownie* cookie — the frowning counterpart to the chain's Smiley Cookie — delivered to the family gathered after Morley's 1998 funeral, recounted in an essay by Alex Frank and still quoted by Dan nearly three decades later. It is one of the most durable food references in the corpus and it is not about a cookie. It is about small-town Western Pennsylvania producing a perfectly timed dark joke at a funeral.

**Metaphor.** "Diet" survives in the corpus mainly in its transferred sense — *media diet*, *information diet* — most visibly around the YouTube watch history. The word outlived the thing it names.

**Body art.** A Misfits skull inside a pizza slice, inked on the forearm on 13 October 2018 at roughly 3" × 1.5" — and the reasoning Dan gives for it, in a November 2018 exchange, is entirely about the band: he did not want the conventional Crimson Ghost variant "where it looks like it's in a coffin with the arms folded," found a pizza-slice reinterpretation, and had it drawn up custom. That reasoning is a provenance-authenticity operation in its purest form — the received image rejected, the sought variant chosen — executed **through** a food image without the food being the point. He has pizza on his arm for life and it is a Misfits tattoo.

## Why: the exemption is stated, not inferred

The mechanism does not have to be guessed at, because the record names it. MAX_PRIME, the framework governing how AI personas must operate, instructs that **"the food and the cat are always real"** — and the record documents what that instruction does: food and the animals are "exempt from the forensic treatment everything else in the record gets."

That is the whole finding in the subject's own words. The forensic apparatus — the same machinery that produced a two-sided Gini coefficient, a message-latency analysis and a decade-long relationship verdict — is explicitly instructed to leave food alone, on the grounds that it is the thing that stays real while everything else is being analysed. **The category is not un-judged by oversight. It is protected.** An axis that would sort food into performed and authentic would make it another object of analysis, and its entire function here is to be the thing analysis does not reach.

## What this does to the master filter

The master filter — the performed-versus-authentic axis applied with unusual rigidity to music, language, politics, film, work and strangers — has a jurisdiction rather than being universal. Food is the high-contact domain that filter has never been pointed at, by an exemption the record states explicitly. "One axis for everything" should be read as "one axis for everything it is allowed to touch."

The gap is wider than it looks, because the filter reaches a long way. His Twitter account shows it turned on the woman he lived with for six years: the warm register there falls away and is replaced by jokes at her expense. A sieve that reaches the most intimate relationship in the record, and still does not reach dinner, is being held off food on purpose rather than for lack of a chance.

## What would prove this wrong

1. **A food judgement about flavour.** If a recorded preference ever surfaces framed purely as *this tastes better*, that is the cleanest falsifier available. The tattoo already proved that a food image can carry a provenance-authenticity judgement — but the judgement was about the band, not the pizza.

1. **A food entry in the favourites record.** The masterlist has held four categories across 2,016 entries; a fifth appearing would show the exemption was a filing habit rather than a protection.

1. **A restaurant or cooking record that reads as taste.** Every documented instance so far has read as logistics or labour. One that reads as flavour would break the pattern.

## Predictions

1. **If a food judgement ever surfaces, it will not be about flavour.** It will sort on region, class, or provenance-authenticity — a master-filter operation wearing a food costume, like the tattoo.

1. **A food entry will never appear in the favourites record.** The four-category structure has held for the entire curated span.

1. **Any future ingest of restaurant or cooking material will read as logistics or as labour**, matching the Caviar and Au Za'atar shape, rather than as taste.

## What we still don't know

- **The message-dump counts are lexical, not semantic.** `food` at 968 includes every use of the word, and the six evaluative hits were found by a fixed phrase list. A proper pass would code a sample of the 968 by register rather than trusting the pattern the samples showed.

- **The dump ends around August 2025.** The terminal phase and the 2026 return — including the whole BFS Foods period — are outside it, so the "no evaluation" claim is strongest for 2015–2025 and weakest for the last year.

- **Nothing here checks in-person or call behaviour**, which is invisible to a text corpus and is exactly where food opinions would ordinarily live. This is the most serious limitation on the page and it cannot be closed from the available source material.

- **The regime's origin is undated.** Whether it predates or follows the adolescent bulimia and candy-staple period is unknown, and the answer decides whether this is a control behaviour that grew out of a disordered one or an independent lifelong trait.
